# Pocket Oracle — Early User Feedback (17 October 2026)

**Stage:** qualitative discovery during the Hacktoberfest 2026 challenge. **Sample:** two individuals. This is **not** a representative survey, accessibility audit, or proof of paid demand.

**Consent and privacy:** Both participants answered the direct question asking permission to quote their opinions anonymously. Participant names, telephone numbers, personal questions and message headers are intentionally omitted. Original comments were in Portuguese; English renderings for an article should be marked as translations. The conversations are kept by the project owner, not reproduced in full here.

## Participant A — Professional tarot reader

- App experience: "O app é fluido e fiel aos significados das cartas, as leituras são interessantes, tem uma linguagem fácil de entender."
- Interpretation: "Os significados são fiéis,a leitura com a IA é de fácil entendimento."
- Professional usefulness: "Ajuda a explicar melhor os significados para pessoas leigas."
- Opinion about audio accessibility: "Sim muito bom, e facilita para melhorar o acesso."
- Would consider paying: "Sim, principalmente a função da leitura."
- Permission for anonymous quotation: "Pode usar sim ."

**Interpretation:** She saw value for practitioners explaining card meanings to beginners, not just automating content production. Her reference to "leitura" is ambiguous between a reading and audio narration; **do not claim she specifically preferred the ElevenLabs feature**. She gave positive feedback about accessibility as a concept; the interview does not verify that a blind person used or could operate the app.

## Participant B — General-user perspective

- Overall usability and AI interpretation: "É legal"
- Would consider paying: "Não sei é legal acho que sim"
- Opinion about audio accessibility or other accessibility improvements: "Não sei não"
- Permission for anonymous quotation: "Tá"

**Interpretation:** Her reaction to the app was positive but brief. Her willingness to pay is **uncertain/tentative**, not a confirmed yes. She did not offer an assessment of accessibility. Do not present her answers as a usability test, proof that audio was exercised, or an endorsement from a tarot professional.

## What we can fairly report

1. One professional evaluator found the reading text understandable and reported a plausible communication use case.
2. The second evaluator responded positively to the general experience, but with limited detail.
3. The professional explicitly said she would consider paying for a reading-related function; the general user was unsure.
4. Both allowed anonymous use of their comments.
5. Neither interview constitutes verification of compliance with WCAG or actual usability by blind users.

## Next steps

- Seek one additional independent evaluator if time permits, without treating three as a statistically meaningful sample.
- Request feedback using the short Microsoft Forms survey to standardize the tested features and distinguish actual audio use from opinions about potential accessibility.
- Test theme, native read-aloud, keyboard navigation and screen-reader compatibility before claiming accessibility improvements as validated.
- In DEV.to, identify any English quotations as translations from Portuguese, and disclose that the sample size is two (or update the number only after additional responses).
